# Swiss Style / International Typographic Style

## What it is
Swiss Style, also known as the International Typographic Style, is a modernist design movement that became popular in Switzerland during the 1950s. It focuses on clean layouts, strong typography, grid systems, simplicity, and clear communication. The goal is to make information easy to understand without using unnecessary decoration.

## Main Features
- Grid-based layouts
- Sans-serif typography
- Strong alignment
- Lots of white space
- Simple geometric shapes
- Minimal decoration
- Clear visual hierarchy
- Functional and organized design

## Why I Chose It
I chose Swiss Style because it looks clean, modern, and professional without being too dramatic. I like how it keeps everything organized while still making the design visually attractive. It feels simple but not boring, which is why I think it works well for websites, posters, and professional branding.

## Historical Example 1
### Josef Müller-Brockmann – Musica Viva Poster
Josef Müller-Brockmann is one of the designers most closely connected with Swiss Style. His Musica Viva posters use strong grid systems, geometric shapes, clean spacing, and simple typography. The design is minimal, but the layout still feels energetic and visually interesting.

## Historical Example 2
### Armin Hofmann – Giselle Poster
Armin Hofmann’s Giselle poster is another strong example of Swiss Style. It uses black-and-white contrast, simple typography, and a highly organized composition. The design shows how Swiss Style can create a strong visual impact without using excessive decoration.

## How I Would Apply It
I would use Swiss Style for a modern website or portfolio by using a clean grid, large sans-serif headings, simple colors, and plenty of white space. I would keep the layout organized and easy to scan so the user can quickly understand the information.
